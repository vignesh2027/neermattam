# Real-video flow speed

What this is: the flow-speed feature (`perception/src/neermattam_perception/flow.py` and `velocity.py`) run end to end on short real flood videos from Wikimedia Commons, through the same `Pipeline.measure(video=...)` an upload goes through. Sources, licences and the exact segments are in `eval/video_sources.json`; rerun with

```
cd perception
uv run --extra local python ../eval/real_videos.py
```

How a speed is made: Muse Spark Open reads the key frame (the sharpest of the first 30 frames) and the verify-and-refine agent measures a reference, a wheel or a person, which gives the scale in centimetres per pixel. Muse Spark Open then boxes up to eight floating objects and one fixed anchor, and SAM 2.1's video predictor tracks every box forward for up to 4 s at about 10 frames per second. Tracks that are lost, change size wildly, do not move, go against the common direction or sit far from the median are set aside, each with its reason. When the anchor is tracked cleanly its motion is subtracted. The surface speed is the median of the kept tracks; the depth-averaged speed is 0.85 times that (docs/GEOMETRY.md, section 6).

What it is not: nobody measured the water in these videos, so nothing below is an accuracy figure. The known-motion check tests the tracker and the arithmetic on synthetic clips; the plausible-range check only says a number is not absurd. A clip with no measured reference gets no speed in metres per second; its tracks are still reported in pixels per second.

Known limits: only motion across the image is seen, so flow towards or away from the camera is missed; the scale is right at the reference's distance and wrong for debris much nearer or farther; the anchor removes image shifts, not zoom or a camera that walks along the street.
